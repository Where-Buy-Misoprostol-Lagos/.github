# Where to get misoprostol in Lagos: the safe routes, and what to avoid

**Last reviewed: 17 November 2026.** This is an independent information page.
We do not sell medicines, take orders, or accept payment from sellers.

> **Emergency?** If you are bleeding heavily, have a fever, or have severe pain,
> go to the nearest hospital now, or call the free Lagos emergency lines
> **112** or **767**. See [warning signs](#warning-signs-go-to-a-hospital-now).

## The short answer

- Misoprostol is a prescription medicine. The safe route is a doctor or a
  licensed clinic, and then a **registered pharmacy** with a pharmacist on site.
- You can check any pharmacy in Lagos on the Pharmacy Council of Nigeria's
  public register before you buy. [How to check](#1-check-the-pharmacy).
- You can check the product itself with NAFDAC, the Nigerian medicines
  regulator. [How to check](#2-check-the-product).
- Do not buy from a social media account, a WhatsApp number, a street seller,
  or a website that will not show its pharmacy registration. Fake and
  badly stored tablets are common in these channels, and nobody is there to
  help you if something goes wrong.
- If you need advice first, MSI Nigeria Reproductive Choices runs a free,
  confidential phone line: **22252** or **0800 00 22252**.

## What misoprostol is

Misoprostol is a medicine that protects the stomach lining and makes the womb
contract. Doctors use it for several purposes:

| Use | Source |
|---|---|
| Preventing and treating heavy bleeding after childbirth | [WHO Essential Medicines List][eml] |
| Treating an incomplete miscarriage | [WHO Essential Medicines List][eml] |
| Together with mifepristone, for medical abortion and after the death of a fetus in the womb | [WHO Essential Medicines List][eml] |
| Lowering the risk of stomach ulcers caused by pain medicines such as ibuprofen or aspirin | [Product label][label] |

The right dose, and the right way to take it, depend on the reason for use,
how many weeks pregnant a person is, and their medical history. This page does
not give doses. A doctor, nurse, midwife or pharmacist must give them to you
for your own situation.

## Who is at higher risk

The official product label carries these warnings ([product label][label]):

- **Pregnancy.** Misoprostol can cause miscarriage, premature birth and birth
  defects. The miscarriage can be incomplete, which can lead to dangerous
  bleeding and may need hospital treatment.
- **Later pregnancy or a previous caesarean section.** Misoprostol can tear
  the womb (uterine rupture). The risk rises as pregnancy advances, and after
  earlier surgery on the womb, including a caesarean. A tear can cause severe
  bleeding and death.
- **Heart or blood vessel disease.** The label advises caution.
- **Never share your tablets.** The label warns that the medicine can be
  dangerous for another person, especially if she is pregnant.

Tell the doctor or pharmacist about every one of these that applies to you.

## What the law says in Nigeria

This is a summary, not legal advice.

- The Federal Ministry of Health states that Nigerian law permits ending a
  pregnancy only where continuing it threatens the life of the mother
  ([National Guidelines on Safe Termination of Pregnancy for Legal
  Indications][fmoh]).
- Lagos is covered by the Criminal Code. Sections 228 to 230 set prison terms
  of up to 14 years for a person who unlawfully tries to cause a miscarriage,
  up to 7 years for a woman who unlawfully tries to cause her own, and up to
  3 years for a person who supplies drugs knowing they are for that purpose
  ([same guideline][fmoh]).
- These rules are one reason so many unlicensed sellers operate in secret.
  Secrecy protects the seller, not you.

If a doctor decides you need misoprostol for a lawful medical reason, a
hospital or licensed clinic can prescribe it and supply it properly.

## How to get it safely in Lagos

### Start with a qualified provider

- **A government general hospital or teaching hospital.** Go to the obstetrics
  and gynaecology clinic, or to the emergency unit if it is urgent.
- **A licensed private clinic or hospital.**
- **MSI Nigeria Reproductive Choices** (formerly Marie Stopes Nigeria), a
  registered reproductive health provider with clinics in Lagos. Call free on
  **22252** or **0800 00 22252**, or message on WhatsApp at
  **+234 909 902 2252**. Clinic addresses are on the
  [official website][msi]. These numbers were checked against the
  [official contact page][msi-contact] on 17 November 2026.

### 1. Check the pharmacy

The Pharmacy Council of Nigeria (PCN) registers every lawful pharmacy.

1. Open the [PCN website][pcn] and choose **Verify Premises**.
2. Search for the pharmacy by name.
3. If it is not on the register, do not buy there.

An online pharmacy must also be registered with the PCN, and must display the
council's registered online pharmacy emblem on its website
([review of the Online Pharmacy Regulations 2021][ropse]).

### 2. Check the product

NAFDAC gives you two free checks.

**The scratch code.** Many medicine packs carry a scratch panel. NAFDAC
explains that you scratch it to reveal a one-time PIN, send the PIN by free
text message to the short code printed on the pack, and receive a reply saying
the product is genuine or suspected fake
([NAFDAC Mobile Authentication Service][mas]). The short codes NAFDAC lists are:

| Service provider | Short code |
|---|---|
| PharmaSecure | 38351 |
| Sproxil | 38353 |
| Savanté | 38120 |
| UBQ-t / Kezzler | 20966 |
| M-Pedigree | 1393 |

**The registration number.** Every registered product has a NAFDAC number on
the pack. Search for the product in the [NAFDAC Greenbook][greenbook],
Nigeria's database of registered products. If the product or number is not
there, do not use it.

You can report a suspected fake medicine through the
**Report Substandard/Falsified Medicines** link on the [NAFDAC website][nafdac].

## How to spot an unsafe seller

Walk away if you see any of these:

- The seller is only a phone number, a WhatsApp or Telegram account, or a
  social media page.
- You must pay before delivery, by transfer to a personal account.
- The seller cannot name a registered pharmacy that you can find on the PCN
  register.
- No one asks about your health, your pregnancy, or your medical history.
- Loose tablets, a cut blister strip, no box, no leaflet, no batch number, no
  expiry date, or no NAFDAC number.
- The scratch code is missing, already scratched, or returns "suspected fake".
- The seller offers a "guarantee", a "100% success" claim, or a dose by chat.
- The price is far below what registered pharmacies charge.

A photograph of a pack proves nothing. Packaging is easy to copy, so use the
NAFDAC checks above.

## Warning signs: go to a hospital now

The World Health Organization tells women to return to a hospital or clinic
after an abortion or miscarriage if they have any of these
([WHO clinical practice handbook][who-handbook]):

- **Heavy bleeding:** soaking more than two pads an hour for two hours in a row.
- **Fever.** WHO advises assessment if a fever lasts more than 24 hours after
  the last dose of misoprostol.
- **Pain or cramping that is getting worse**, or severe pain that does not stop.
- **Signs that the pregnancy is continuing**, such as nausea, vomiting, breast
  tenderness or tiredness.

Also seek care quickly if you had **very little bleeding or no bleeding**.
WHO advises clinicians to check for a continuing pregnancy or an ectopic
pregnancy (a pregnancy outside the womb), which is an emergency.

In Lagos, the free emergency lines are **112** and **767**
([Lagos State Government][lagos]). Treatment for bleeding, infection and pain
is medical care. Tell the staff exactly what you took and when, so that they
can treat you correctly.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does misoprostol cost in Nigeria?**
We do not list prices, because they change and differ between pharmacies. Ask
a registered pharmacy. Treat a very low price, or a demand for payment before
delivery, as a warning sign.

**How should misoprostol be taken?**
That depends on why it is prescribed, how many weeks pregnant a person is, and
their medical history. A dose that is right for one person can be dangerous
for another. Get the instructions from the provider who prescribes it. The
[WHO handbook][who-handbook] is the reference that health workers use.

**Can misoprostol fail?**
Yes. The product label states that a miscarriage caused by misoprostol can be
incomplete, and that birth defects have been reported after unsuccessful use
in early pregnancy ([product label][label]). If you think it did not work, see
a clinician. Do not take more tablets on your own.

**Can misoprostol damage the womb?**
The label warns of uterine rupture, a tear in the womb. The risk rises later
in pregnancy and after a caesarean or other surgery on the womb
([product label][label]). This is why a provider needs to know how far along
the pregnancy is before anything is prescribed.

**I took misoprostol and there is little or no bleeding. What should I do?**
See a clinician promptly. WHO advises checking for a continuing pregnancy or
an ectopic pregnancy ([WHO handbook][who-handbook]).

**I took misoprostol and the bleeding is very heavy. What should I do?**
If you soak more than two pads an hour for two hours in a row, go to a
hospital immediately or call 112 or 767.

**What side effects are common?**
Cramping, bleeding, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and fever or chills are the
side effects WHO lists ([WHO handbook][who-handbook]).

**Is unsafe abortion really dangerous?**
Yes. WHO states that around 45% of abortions worldwide are unsafe, and that
unsafe abortion can cause incomplete abortion, heavy bleeding, infection and
injury to internal organs ([WHO fact sheet][who-fact]).

**Who can I talk to in confidence?**
Call MSI Nigeria Reproductive Choices free on 22252 or 0800 00 22252. You can
also speak to a doctor, nurse, midwife or pharmacist.
